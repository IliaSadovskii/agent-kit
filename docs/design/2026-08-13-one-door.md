# One door into the knowledge, and completeness instead of a route

Written 2026-08-13, after a live `blueprint` on the one real project went wrong in a way that turned
out not to be a bug in a rule. The owner had used the product, had a list of things that did not
match, typed `blueprint` with no argument, and got a six-phase interview. Two rules were patched the
same day — that is 2.7.1 — and the patch did not reach the cause.

## The cause

The kit has **five ways in** and the owner has one thought: *I want to tell you something about my
product.*

| Today | |
|---|---|
| `blueprint` | continue whatever is unclosed, along a route the command chooses |
| `blueprint <what to add>` | the owner brings a thought |
| `blueprint <what did not match>` | the owner brings a complaint |
| `blueprint --recall [part]` | read it back |
| `blueprint --check` | the program's summary |

The middle two are **the same door**, and `SKILL.md` says so in as many words: *"the same door from
the other side"*. Which one an owner is at is decided by where the thought came from — imagined or
observed — and nothing downstream cares. The first is also the same door, entered by somebody who
did not know a door needed a name.

So three of the five are one thing wearing three labels, and the label is chosen by the owner, who
is the one person who cannot be expected to know the difference.

## The second cause: a route where a checklist belongs

The interview is six phases, ordered, and a run announces which one it is in. That is right for an
empty repository: the vocabulary of the early phases is what makes the later ones answerable, and
somebody describing a product they have not built has nothing to offer but the order.

It is wrong for every project after that. An owner with a running product does not have phases; they
have whatever they were thinking about on the way home. Forcing the route means asking them to sort
their own thoughts into the kit's order before speaking — which is exactly what this command already
refuses to do at a smaller scale, in its own words: *walking the slots asks the owner to translate
into a structure that is the kit's convenience*.

The phases are not wrong as a **list of what must exist**. They are wrong as a **path to walk**.

## What replaces both

One door. The owner talks — a sentence, a part, the whole product, a list of complaints, in any
order, at any time. Then five steps, and they are the same five whatever was said and however much
of the project already exists:

1. **They talk.** Open question, no options. Whatever they give is the input.
2. **Read what is already written** on the topics they touched.
3. **Put the reading up before writing anything**: this is new, this refines that, and here you
   contradict what is recorded — which is right? Contradictions are the only part that must be
   answered before the writing happens.
4. **Write and commit.**
5. **Then the gaps**, and only then — asked as choices, in the order below.

An empty project has an empty step 2 and a long step 5; a mature one is the reverse; a dictation
about one part narrows both to that part. It is one machine at three sizes, which is what makes it
smaller than what it replaces.

## Step 3 is the one that can be faked

Comparing free prose against fifty-two entries means reading fifty-two entries. The cheap path is to
read a third, find something, and report confidently — and *"I found three differences"* is exactly
what an honest pass and a lazy one both produce.

So the reading is reported the way the audit lenses report theirs: **one line per record touched,
including the ones that did not change**, each naming the entry. A short report about a long
dictation is then visibly short, which is the only property that survives contact with a session
that would rather be finished. This kit has learned the same lesson three times, and it is written
down in `docs/design/method.md`: name the cheapest way to produce plausible output, then demand an
artefact that path cannot produce.

## Which gaps are asked about, and which are not

Two kinds, found by two different things.

**A field that is not there** is the program's job and is already done: `check.py` compares each
record against the templates and prints what is missing, what has no verdict, which parts were never
walked. No session has to look for those.

**A field that is filled and says nothing** is the session's job, and it needs a filter or it
produces forty questions and teaches the owner to tap without reading. The filter already exists in
this command and is kept unchanged — what the person sees, what happens when it fails, who may not,
what costs money, what a thing ends with; never how it is stored, which index, which schema. It has
a test attached for the borderline cases: *can the code be wrong about this without anyone
noticing?* A screen's existence, no. A scenario's ending, yes, for months.

Roles stay on the asking side of that line and architecture stays off it. Nothing but the owner can
say who may **not** do a thing — the code shows who can, and never says whether that was intended.
How it is layered is the build's decision and is recorded, not asked.

And the asking is bounded by cost, in three tiers:

1. **What blocks `epic`** — the MVP bounds, the scenarios and their endings. Its gate refuses to
   start without them, so a gap here is not a nicety.
2. **What is expensive to get wrong** — stored shapes, money, permissions.
3. **Everything else is not asked at all.** It is taken, recorded as an assumption, and shown in one
   list at the end: *here is what I decided for you*. That third tier is the whole difference between
   a command that clarifies and one that interrogates.

## What does not change

Nearly all of it, and deliberately — the parts and their `walked:` marks, the draft-what-the-code-
can-witness table, the document-is-a-witness rule, the older-kit path, how a session ends and
commits, the blocks. Those are paid-for rules and none of them is what went wrong.

`--recall` stays a separate door because it is a different verb: it writes nothing. `--check` stays
because it is a program.

## What this costs

Re-reading knowledge on every substantial dictation. It is the price of the thing being asked for:
a description can only be kept current against the previous version of itself, and there is no
cheaper way to know whether a sentence contradicts one written in March. The alternative — trusting
that the owner remembers what they said last time — is what produced the fifty-two entries carrying
seventy assumptions nobody has read.
